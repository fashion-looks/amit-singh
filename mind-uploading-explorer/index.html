01 / The concept

What is whole-brain emulation?

A hypothetical process that would map the brain's relevant structure and dynamics, then build a computational model intended to reproduce its function. A detailed scan alone would not automatically reveal every connection, chemical state, or rule needed for a faithful simulation.

02 / Two hypothetical routes

Two paths to a digital self

📡

Scan and emulate

Create a model from measurements of a biological brain. It is unknown whether current or foreseeable technology could capture everything necessary.

🧩

Gradual replacement

A thought experiment in which biological components are replaced by functional artificial counterparts. Whether this preserves subjective continuity is unresolved.

04 / Possible implications

Benefits people imagine

  • New ways to study cognition and brain disorders.
  • Potential digital agents that model some aspects of an individual.
  • Speculative long-duration space missions without a biological body.
These are possibilities discussed in futurism, not demonstrated outcomes. Digital immortality and conscious uploads have not been achieved.
05 / The hard problem

Would a copy still be “you”?

A model might reproduce memories and behaviour without answering whether it has subjective experience—or whether the original person's point of view continues.
